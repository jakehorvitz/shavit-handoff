The one-line thesis, why his edit looks professional and ours did not

His base layer is real gimbal walkthrough footage, and the craft is speed ramps locked to music beats + motion blur over every ramp + a named transition at every seam. AI appears constantly (night exterior, furnished rooms, dolly videos) but is never shown slow, it flashes past at 300-1600% speed under blur, so your eye reads energy, not seams. Our failed tours did the opposite: long, slow AI glides with nothing to hide them. His recipe = exactly the fix for every note you left on preview_tour v1-v3.

Music structure

Every single speed point, cut, and transition midpoint is dragged onto a beat, he says it out loud a dozen times ("right between the last beat and the beat drop", "right over top of the beat"). The edit is built music-first: beat grid down, then footage bent to it. For us: lock the Hurts So Good full-band section first, mark every backbeat, then place ramps. This is the single highest-leverage habit in the whole video.

Technique cards, exact settings, cited

T1 · Speed ramp (the core unit) 0:28-1:55, used everywhere

  • Ctrl+R on the clip → add a speed point → slow segment to 50% (or 4-5% for a near-freeze before an AI insert; up to 1600% for whips).
  • Speed marker placed between the last beat and the beat drop; fast segment ends exactly on the drop.
  • Keyframe tray → switch lanes to curves → select keyframe → ease in and out → drag handle out; when it hits the clip edge, hold Shift and extend the other side further. That asymmetric easing is what makes his ramps feel liquid.

T2 · Day → night exterior (AI) 1:55-3:49

  • Grab a still of the real day shot (color page → grab still → export).
  • Higgsfield image tab, upload still, prompt verbatim:
Keep the photo exactly the same. Turn the photo to nighttime. Turn on the interior lights so the windows are glowing. Turn on the exterior lights.
  • Then Cinema Studio: start frame = that generation, camera movement = dolly in, no text prompt, aspect 9:16, duration 10s (extra runway for ramping), audio off, slow motion off.
  • This is exactly our approved V_entry2 pattern, one clean camera primitive from a real still, no prompt.

T3 · Stabilize an AI clip 3:49-4:38

  • Fusion: Tracker node over a high-contrast center feature → track forward + reverse.
  • Operation = match move, merge = background only. Add a Transform, keyframe center at frame 1, then scale up until the transparent edges vanish.
  • Fixes the AI camera-shake complaint without rejecting the clip.

T4 · Luma fade transition 4:38-7:09 · his default seam

  • Settings he uses "99% of the time": luma source = video 2, fade mode = bright first. The bright windows eat the frame first, that is why day→night feels organic.
  • Midpoint tuning: hold Ctrl on the left edge of the transition and drag left, extends it AND shifts the midpoint early.
  • He also uses a luma fade to exit the furnished still (15:10), reads as a wipe, not a dissolve. This answers your "needs to be more creative than a dissolve" note.

T5 · Fake speed ramp on an adjustment clip 6:19-7:07, 32:08

  • Adjustment clip over the seam → Fusion Transform → keyframe size 1 → 2, spline S-handles bent so the growth all lands at the end.
  • Adds camera energy to clips that have none, directly usable on our static photo crops.

T6 · AI furnishing still 9:09-10:33

  • Still of the exact freeze frame → Higgsfield Nano Banana Pro → prompt verbatim:
Furnish the living room with modern furniture. Add a couch on the left, two accent chairs by the large windows, a modern light stand, a coffee table, an area rug. Make the furniture elegant and modern. Do not change anything about the perspective or proportions of the room, and add a TV on the TV wall and a TV console.
  • Note the shape: named objects with placements + a hard "do not change perspective or proportions", our realism-prompt standard already matches this.
  • 2K resolution, aspect matched. Because it is the same frame, the cut lands pixel-true.

T7 · Disappear effect + freeze cut 13:48-15:10

  • Slow the real clip to ~4%, cut, place the furnished still after the cut, keep the tail of the real clip to cut back to the same frame.
  • The Disappear generator sits over the cut (offset zeroed with , and . keys), pivot moved over the furniture, aspect widened. Furniture materializes with an aberration flicker instead of a crossfade.

T8 · Shine sweep on a feature 16:07-19:41

  • Fusion: Tracker in corner positioning mode pinned to the stovetop, tracked only between two timeline markers.
  • White Background node piped into the tracker foreground input, masked by a thin vertical rectangle, huge soft edge, slight rotation, keyframed to sweep left → right across the stove. Easing ~80, blend 0.07 so it is a glint, not a flashlight.
  • This is a "feature callout" without a caption, could be our stainless / island / shower-glass beat and it answers "describe the rooms" without words.

T9 · Furnishing cycle + wobble 19:48-22:29

  • 3-4 furnishing variants of one frame, straight cuts on beats.
  • Adjustment clip over all of it → Fusion Camera Shake: deviations near zero, overall strength keyframed 0→1→0 spiking exactly at each cut, edges = mirror, shake speed ~0.04, eased with sharpened peaks.
  • The cut itself is what shakes, reads like an impact, not a slideshow advance. Directly reusable over our photo cuts.

T10 · Fridge / door pop 22:29-27:09

  • Two real takes (closed walk-in, open walk-in), both reversed, both speed ramped to ~50% with the stop landing between beats.
  • Alignment trick: overlay clip 2 at opacity 0 to eyeball the match; both clips tracker-stabilized (match move, background only) and centered against the center guide; a throwaway rectangle at the fridge top is copy-pasted between Fusion comps as a scale reference.
  • Seam covered by a Warp transition, mode strong warp 2, strength ≈1.38, a tiny twist-flick, not a full warp.

T11 · Dolly stack (empty → furnished ×N) 27:09-31:17

  • One furnished still → Cinema Studio dolly-in, repeated 3-4 times from the SAME start frame → near-identical camera paths.
  • Sync in the edit: overlay at opacity 0, raise speed until features line up (~300%), trim, cut between them mid-move.
  • Seams: warp transitions alternating left, right, left, value ≈0.15, easing changed expo → sine (softer). Alternation = rhythm; never repeat one gimmick twice in a row, your standing rule, confirmed by the reference.

T12 · Cine whip + motion blur pass 32:40-40:10

  • Cine whip into the island orbit: transition as long as possible, direction right + slightly up (matches the orbit), curve mode overshoot (eases in, snaps back out), additional crop raised to hide edge mirroring. Built-in blur, never double-blur a whip.
  • Then a blur pass over every speed ramp with Motion Blur Simple: zoom mode scale ≈1.7 for push-ins, ≈−1.5 for pull-outs, direction mode (scale 1, angle 0, low strength) for lateral moves, pivot point ON the subject. Spans shortened and centered on each ramp. Pro (pixel-tracking) only for curvy moves.
  • His words: motion blur is "the one major critical piece missing" from most edits. It was also missing from every cut of ours.

Why our parked cuts failed his test

Your note (on tour v1-v3)What the reference does instead
"too slow… the door needs to open into the house"Nothing slow ever survives; slow segments exist only as launch pads for a ramp landing on a beat.
"that cut scene SCREAMS AI… less staged, less shiny"AI shown at 300%+ under motion blur, or as a 1-frame-matched still. Never asked to carry a scene.
"cut too sudden… look at real estate tours"Every seam has a named device: luma fade, warp .15, cine whip, disappear, shine. Zero naked cuts.
"weird jump cut… should be smooth one shot"The "one shot" feel comes from motion continuity across cuts (reverse ramps + whips that match direction), not from actual long takes.
"WHAT THE FUCK IS THIS THIS IS NOT THE HOUSE"Furnishing prompts pin perspective and proportions; dolly videos are generated FROM the real still, so the geometry never drifts.
