## 8/25 12:44 PM — STORYTELLING DOCTRINE (call + full copy rewrite; deck rev 15)

Shavit approved design and photos ("seriously looks great") and rejected the copy
register, then rewrote all nine slides himself (iMessage 12:44 PM, on the deck
verbatim). The doctrine, in his words from the call — this is the standing brief
for EVERY future Shavit deliverable, not a one-post note:

1. **Never transcribe the photo.** If the viewer can see it, the caption saying it
   is dead weight ("condensers outside... well, duh. That's what we're showing
   with the photo"). The photo carries the WHAT; the copy carries the WHY.
2. **Copy sells the competitive advantage, market-specifically.** Name what the
   market does not do and what CPM does ("Most rental homes in Hillsdale do not
   include air conditioning... Comfort should not be considered luxury").
3. **No comparisons, no "better than."** "A Porsche doesn't need to say I'm better
   than this. I can just focus on myself and speak the language for people to
   see." State the standard; never punch at competitors.
4. **No repetition across projects.** Photo-transcription copy makes every
   completed-project post read the same. Advantage-led copy is what
   differentiates post N from post N+1.
5. **Think brand strategist, not designer.** His framing: we were producing as
   designers; the ask is brand strategy. Design/photos were already right — the
   gap was messaging.
