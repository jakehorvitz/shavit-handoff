## Strategy against the Facebook playbook

**The audience here is the block.** Neighbours, Cleveland Heights community
groups, local tradespeople, sellers with a similar building nearby. So the post
opens on his COMMUNITY IMPACT line ("Returned a vacant and underperforming
multifamily property to productive use in Cleveland Heights.") and spends its
last third on the rest of that section, which is the only part of his docx
written to the neighbourhood rather than to an investor.

**Praise the street; the house is the villain.** His words do it: "a prime
location serving the greater University Circle employment and educational
corridor" versus "a vacant and underperforming multifamily property". The
building's condition is the antagonist; the neighbourhood is never knocked.

**Long narrative, 150 to 300 words.** About 255. Open on the human beat, the
location, the exterior (the one section the carousel barely shows), the work,
what the block got back, the hold, his "homes that we would be proud to live in
ourselves", the end card. Written so a group admin could reshare it verbatim: no
figures, no link, no hashtag spam.

**Stagger and lead with a different beat.** The carousel opens on his one-liner
(or L72) and goes room by room; this opens on impact and exterior. Instagram
Sunday 8/23, Facebook Monday 8/24.

**Photos.** Five real photographs, exterior first, then rooms, leading with
frames the carousel does not use (kitchen toward the back door, front room
looking straight out at the street, the attic kitchen).

**Tag the city page, never an address.** The City of Cleveland Heights page is
tagged in the composer; the street name appears (his L43 with the house number
removed), the house number never does.

**Fair housing.** The people sentences are his (L69, "medical residents and
other professionals relocating to Cleveland from outside the region"), who did
lease, never who should.
