3 · Brand specialist, not designer

The standard is Shavit's own 8/28 9:00 PM correction, and his reasoning was operational rather than aesthetic:

I don't think it was possible to not see how distressed 34 Mead was. We just have better funding, operations, on site management, and Renters pipeline to be able to execute projects that other people will pass on. In other words, it is not about we fix what other people don't see, it is about we do what others cannot. Shavit · 8/28 9:00 PM

So the caption never describes the photographs. The photos carry the transformation; the copy carries the advantage. The advantage beat is his own sentence from the BUY step, pulled forward into paragraph three:

Because we specialize in value-add investments, we were able to move quickly
and purchase the home directly.

That is the same claim as "we do what others cannot", stated with a fact instead of a slogan. Which matters, because he rejected the slogan at 9:18 PM the same night: Jake proposed WE INVEST WHERE OTHERS WALK AWAY and WE DO WHAT OTHERS CANNOT, and the reply was "Nope! Negative for both." Neither line belongs on a slide or in this caption.

The hook is the acquisition, not the renovation

Every operator in Hillsdale posts a finished kitchen. Almost none can say a homeowner called them because of a mailer. His L35 line is the most differentiated sentence in the whole case study, and it does double duty: character to a tenant or seller, deal flow to a lender or partner. It leads the body.

The numbers stay on the website

Acquisition price, renovation spend, ARV, equity, monthly rent, cap rate and ROI are all barred by no-dollar and uncited-numeral. The caption does not gesture at them vaguely either. It names them as the reason to click, in his own inventory sentence ("the economics behind the investment"). The promise of the number moves traffic to the case study he just spent a reel announcing; the number itself would only satisfy curiosity in the feed.

Mead Street. Hillsdale, Michigan.

After receiving one of our "We Buy Houses" mailers distributed throughout Hillsdale, the homeowner contacted us. She was experiencing significant financial hardship, the home had deteriorated over many years, and its condition prevented her from obtaining homeowners insurance.

Rather than seeing a property beyond repair, we saw an opportunity to solve a difficult situation while preserving another home for the Hillsdale community. Because we specialize in value-add investments, we were able to move quickly and purchase the home directly.

The renovation included structural and deferred maintenance repairs, new drywall throughout, new flooring, a completely renovated kitchen, an updated bathroom, new lighting and fixtures, interior and exterior paint, updated mechanical systems, and landscaping and exterior improvements. Rather than applying cosmetic upgrades, the goal was to create a durable, low-maintenance home that would perform well for years to come.

Standardizing materials and finishes improves efficiency while reducing future maintenance costs.

Today, the property is professionally managed, fully occupied, and provides quality housing for a local family. Our goal is not simply to renovate homes. It is to provide housing that tenants are proud to call home.

Capital returned through refinancing was reinvested into additional acquisitions, allowing us to continue expanding our portfolio while preserving quality housing throughout Hillsdale.

Beyond the finished renovation, the case study walks through how we found the opportunity, why we pursued it, the economics behind the investment, the renovation process, the timeline, and the lessons we learned along the way. Read it at ShavitRootman.com/case-studies.

15 · Slide 1 · the landscaping, before and after

Shavit's note 1, actioned, then Jake: "fix the patchy grass". Two passes, neither of them generative. Every green pixel added here is real grass cloned from elsewhere in this same photograph, so the species, the sun angle, the time of day and the camera all match by construction.

  1. Fill. The strip in front of the porch was mostly bare soil, not dying grass, so recolouring could not fix it: there was nothing there to green up. Grass is cloned in from the purest grass patch in the frame (found by search, 98.6% pure, the foreground strip beside the driveway), shrunk to 0.60 for the depth difference so the blades are the right size, and then relit per channel to the brightness and hue the lawn's own grass already has at that spot, which is what carries the sun and shade across the lawn. Matching it to the raw image instead blew the fill out neon, because bare soil is far brighter than grass.
  2. Even out. A light pass over the dry grass that was already there.
  3. Evenness. Patching only the bare spots still read as patchy, because the surviving thatch and the lawn's own blotchy shading were most of what made it look patchy in the first place. So the fill now resurfaces the whole lawn rather than spot-filling it, over a light field blurred wide enough to keep the real sun gradient and drop the blotches.
  4. No repeat. One donor tiled across the lawn repeats visibly, and mirroring it to hide that just trades the repeat for chevrons. The fill is now randomised quilting: four separate donor patches, each ≥99% pure, cross-faded through a Hann window at random offsets and random flips. The donor is also sharpened after its depth rescale, since shrinking softened the blades and the fill read as a smudge against the sharp real grass beside it.

The fill is confined to a hand-drawn polygon over the front lawn, kept deliberately inside the real lawn on every side. Pavement, the porch block, the stone veneer, the plant bed and the house are not reachable by the script at all, whatever the colour test does.

Measured, not asserted. Against a strength-0 control that isolates the JPEG re-encode: the retouch changes zero pixels above the porch line (largest difference 0/255 — not one pixel). 45,630 pixels move by more than 25/255; 91% are inside the lawn polygon and the rest is the hue pass on real grass elsewhere on the ground plane. Nothing above y=700 changes at all, and the house, porch and veneer all sit above that line. The frame is encoded once, not twice, so the house carries a single re-save and nothing else.

Both exteriors on the deck now carry this pass. Since rev 18 slide 8 is exterior-after-clean.jpg, the tight front frame run through the same polygon fill, so his "photos" plural is satisfied. exterior-wide-clean.jpg above stays built and is one line away in render_mead.py if the wider closer is wanted instead.
