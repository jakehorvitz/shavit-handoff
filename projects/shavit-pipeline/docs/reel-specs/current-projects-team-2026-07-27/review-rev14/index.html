The film

What changed

One rule, flipped. Before, a photo stayed on top after it parked, so a corner thumbnail was sitting over every full-screen beat that followed. Now a photo drops to the back the moment it lands in its corner. Three consequences:

• A full-screen photo owns the whole frame, uninterrupted, exactly as you asked.
• The corners accumulate underneath, hidden.
• When the last photo settles, all four appear together — which turns out to be the best moment in the block, because the grid arrives as a reveal instead of being assembled in front of you.

How Oak runs

  1. Ladder room owns the whole screen, then settles top-left — exposing the green room, already full screen behind it.
  2. Green room owns the screen, clean, then settles top-right — exposing the arch.
  3. Arch to the front door owns the screen, clean, then settles bottom-left — exposing the kitchen.
  4. Kitchen owns the screen last (strongest photo, so it gets the final beat), then settles bottom-right — and the other three appear from underneath at the same instant.
  5. All four hold, each still panning, until the white flash into Indiana.

The Oak block, frame by frame

Still true, and still what makes it read as natural. The frame is 9:16 and every corner cell is 9:16, so full screen to corner is a pure uniform scale — nothing warps, nothing re-crops, no aspect snaps at the end. And nothing ever enters: each photo shrinks away to expose one already sitting there, so there is not a single frame of empty background in the clip. Easing is ease-in-out cubic, at rest to at rest, no overshoot.

Read before you sign off

The one real cost, unchanged. Four equal cells in a 9:16 frame forces each cell to 9:16 too, so a 3:4 photo gives up 25% of its width once it is in a corner. Nothing important is lost, and the full-screen beat before each settle uses the same crop, so you always see the photo at full size first.
Runtime headroom is nearly gone. The film is 59.20s and the music bed is 59.40s — six frames spare. Anything that makes this longer needs either a longer bed or a trim, and Norwood's third shot at 1.9s is the flagged candidate.
