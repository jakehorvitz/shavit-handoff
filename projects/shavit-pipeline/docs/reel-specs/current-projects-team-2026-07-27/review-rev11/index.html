"I can't have blurred bars… no blurred bars at all" The grid's defocused backdrop plate is gone entirely. Four 9:16 cells tile 1080×1920 exactly, so at rest every pixel is photograph. The cost: each 3:4 photo gives up 25% of its width, so the crop anchors were picked per photo off a three-way comparison rather than defaulted to centre.
"the grid looks weird, get some movement for each picture" Every tile now runs its own camera the whole clip — two push in, two pull back, pans alternating so no two are synchronised. The pans are real resolution, not a fake: they spend the same 25% strip the crop discards, so nothing is upscaled past 1:1. A 3px white seam was added so four unrelated rooms read as four, not as one broken photograph.
"make the transitions fasts" Blooms 12f → 8f, dip to black 24f → 16f, Barry's dissolve 14f → 10f. Oak's tiles arrive in 12 frames instead of 20 and leave in 7 instead of 12.

A correction to what I told you on rev10. I said the Cedar → Ludlam dissolve buried Cedar's generated wide end. Checking the actual frames, that was wrong: Cedar descends, so its invented neighbourhood rooftops are at its head, not its tail. A dissolve at the tail never covered them and could not have. What does cover them is the bloom coming into Cedar. That is also why Cedar now hands to Saint Joe on a flash rather than a dissolve — Cedar ends on a high oblique, Saint Joe opens on a low straight-on elevation, so dissolving them would mismatch framing and camera height both.
The Oak crop is a real trade. Filling the frame with four cells means each 3:4 photo loses 25% of its width. Nothing important is gone — the ladder room keeps both windows, the kitchen keeps the base cabinets, sink and faucet, the arch shot keeps the arch and drops a folding chair — but it is a crop, and the alternative was the blurred plate you rejected.
