What it cost, and why

There was no free room, so this was paid for out of two shots. The film was already within six frames of the end of the music bed, and the bed cannot be re-cut longer — it is not the one music source in the repo (I cross-correlated them; they score 0.04, so it is a different track entirely). Slowing Oak by 40 frames therefore had to come from somewhere:

Howder's crew shot: 2.50s → 1.67s. This is the clip with the carpenter clipped by the right frame edge. I re-checked it against the export — he is cut off in every single frame, there is no clean window. So shortening it does not fix the defect, but it does cut the defect's screen time by a third, which is the right direction until it gets regenerated.

Norwood's third shot: 1.90s → 1.33s. The one still photograph in a block whose other two shots move, and the shot I have had flagged as the first trim candidate since rev6.

Both are one-line reverts if you would rather have the length back and keep Oak fast.
Everything else is untouched. Ludlam still opens, the two cross-dissolves are still there, the branded cards are unchanged, and the film came out 2 frames shorter than rev14 — 59.13s against a 59.40s bed, so headroom went from 6 frames to 8.
Still open. Howder's clipped carpenter still needs a regeneration at 7.5 credits against roughly 20 available — that remains the single best spend on this film. Ludlam opens on its only usable photo, which shows gas meters on the side elevation. And the Saint Joe interiors are still unused.
