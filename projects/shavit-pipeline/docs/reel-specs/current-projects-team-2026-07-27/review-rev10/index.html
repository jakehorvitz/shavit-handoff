Read before you sign off

Cedar and Ludlam look alike. Two white houses, blue sky, green lawn, similar sun. Chaining them through a dissolve is the whole idea, but the cost is that for half a second it could read as one house rather than two. The address tags carry the difference. If that bothers you the fix is a 5-frame flash between them, and the chained-descent effect goes away with it.
Oak's grid needed two fixes you cannot see in the source. Inset on all four sides it left a dead band under the address tag that no other shot in the film has, so it now bleeds to both frame edges — which also bought 51% more tile area. And the kitchen tile is white cabinets exactly where the tag sits, so there is now a bottom scrim; without it the type was white on white. The fourth photo (the arch to the front door) was a low-contrast copy and was the visible fault in the grid, so contrast is stretched on that tile only.
Still open, unchanged. Howder's clipped carpenter is the oldest defect in the film — he is cut by the right frame edge for the entire clip and only a regeneration with him centred fixes it, at 7.5 credits against roughly 20 available. 11 Ludlam still has exactly one usable source photo. And the Saint Joe interiors — finished kitchen with the blue range, the baths, the staircase — are the best-photographed assets in the library and remain completely unused.
