Why the current shot is weak

  1. Its plate was never real. The photo is landscape and the film is vertical, so a 9:16 plate had to come from somewhere. Outpainting was tried twice and fabricated property both times — a detached garage, a driveway and a hillside on one attempt, an attached wing with its own roof on the other. Both were refused; 35 credits were not spent. The plate in use instead has the house anchored at the bottom with the sky above extrapolated as a gradient.
  2. That flat gradient sky gave the model nothing to move through. The first 5 seconds of the 10s generation are nearly static — frame-diff 26 at t=5 against 78 at t=10. The clip in the film is the back half, reversed.
  3. So it opens on the weakest frame in the film: a high wide shot whose neighbourhood context does not exist in any photograph of this property.

What your note unlocks

Going tighter removes the entire problem. A 9:16 crop of the real photograph uses the full height of the source and 45% of its width. Nothing is added above, below or to the sides. Zero invented pixels, zero outpaint credits, and no brand-rule exposure. Every previous Cedar attempt failed at the plate stage; this skips that stage entirely.

The trade is resolution: a 631×1122 crop upscales 1.71× to 1080×1920 before it goes to the model. Kling re-renders at its own resolution, so this matters less than it sounds, but the plate is softer than Saint Joe's was.

Pick a move

OptionThe moveWhy it works / riskCredits
1 · RISE
recommended		 Starts low and tight on the steps and front door, rises slowly and pulls back to settle on the full front elevation. Ends on the real photograph's framing. The safest move in the set: it ends where the plate already is, so the model has the least room to invent. It also lands on the house rather than leaving it, which is what the block needs. 17.5
2 · PUSH Starts on the full front elevation and pushes in slowly to the door and steps, drifting up a few degrees so the gable stays in frame. The most "listing" of the three and the most likely to feel calm and expensive. Risk is that a straight push can read as a zoom rather than a camera. 17.5
3 · ARC A shallow lateral arc across the front, maybe 15 degrees, holding the door roughly centred so the porch depth and the railing parallax past each other. The most three-dimensional and the most genuinely "sexy". Highest risk: lateral movement is what makes a model invent the sides of a building, which is exactly how the last two Cedar attempts failed. 17.5
Generate at 10s and cut to 5, always. This is the one lesson that has held across every generated shot in this film. At 5s the model compresses a big move into a small one — it happened to GEN1 in rev6 and again on the first Saint Joe attempt. The fix was never the wording, it was the duration. So the 17.5 credits buys a 10s clip and we cut the best 5 seconds out of it.
