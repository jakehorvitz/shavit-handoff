Sky · built, and recommended against

The better idea that does not survive contact with the photos

Sky removes the band completely. Every frame already has a large quiet surface at the top, the ceiling indoors and the sky outdoors, so the type goes there in ink with the progress rule pinned to the top edge of the frame. Nothing is covered and the whole photograph is visible. It is the exact inverse of East Victoria, whose signature is white type on a dark bottom scrim. On cards 1, 2 and 5 it is the best-looking thing built for this post.

It fails on two of six, and the reason is structural. The renderer picks ink or white from the mean luminance of the region under the text block. A mean is only meaningful when the backdrop is uniform, and on two cards it is not. Card 4: the subline crosses the dark stacked stone chimney breast and disappears. Card 6: “Kendall Street, South Bend, Indiana. Finished.” runs onto the white siding and disappears. Per-line ink selection would not save either one, because the failing lines themselves straddle light and dark. It needs frames with genuinely uniform tops, which these two are not.

Shipping four good cards and two broken ones is worse than shipping six consistent ones, so bone is the recommendation. Sky is kept on the record because it is worth revisiting on a property whose photography suits it.

Rev 11 · the re-skin

What he said, and why it happened

Sunday 10:56am: “It is too identical to E Victoria. I vote to change.” He is right, and the cause is on the record. Rev 10 deliberately rebuilt the type on the East Victoria renderer so the two posts would sit together in the grid. Matching was the goal. He read matching as repetition.

Both posts used one lockup: a bottom anchored dark scrim, a gold underlined kicker, a heavy white uppercase headline, a sentence case support line, a gold wordmark. At thumbnail size in the profile grid there was nothing left to tell them apart. The fix therefore has to land at thumbnail scale, not in the detail.

Change A · the scrim comes off and the type gets a band

The photo now runs full bleed and undarkened in the upper region, and the words sit in a solid near black band beneath it. East Victoria reads as text dissolving into a photograph. This reads as a photograph above a hard edge. That difference survives being shrunk to a grid thumbnail, which is the only place the complaint actually lives.

It also fixes something real. The rev 10 scrim darkened the bottom 52 to 62 percent of every frame, which on these slides is precisely where the ladders, the extraction gear and the opened floor are. The post argues that the hidden work is the work worth paying for, and the treatment was dimming the evidence. Nothing is darkened now, so the per card darken values are gone. The per card brightness lift stays, because the interiors are genuinely dim.

Band geometry. Measured across all six cards and then applied uniformly at 532px, 39 percent of the frame, so the baseline never jumps between slides. Each block is centred optically inside that band, so the short cards do not sit on a slab of dead black.

Change C · step index and progress rule replace the word kickers

OPENING UP and THE MIDDLE and THE SAME ROOM become 01 / 06 through 06 / 06, sitting under a gold rule that fills left to right as the carousel advances. By card six the rule is solid gold across the full width.

This is the change that makes the re-skin look deliberate instead of looking like a different template got grabbed. The post's own claim is that a renovation is a sequence most people never watch. A progress bar states that in the furniture, and it gives a reason to keep swiping that the word kickers never gave. It also removes the last piece of East Victoria's signature, which was the gold underlined kicker word.
