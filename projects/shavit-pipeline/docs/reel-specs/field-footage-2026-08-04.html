Reel A1 · ship first Nothing blocking

We bought it with people still living in it. ~45s

The house was occupied when they won the auction. So they sat down with the tenants first.

"We bought that as a foreclosure, very complicated bid on auction.com. We ended up getting that while it was occupied. Believe it or not, me and my partner, Jonah, ended up coming here, talked to the tenants right here before we stripped that living room. We had a conversation with them right here, and we ended up helping them to be able to afford their next place where they lived." IMG_4680 · 0:19–0:41 · standing in the room where the conversation happened
Why this one first

The carousel slate's section 00 named the four things that made East Victoria work, and the top one was "the people came before the property." This is that, except it is not a design decision — it is what actually happened, told by the person it happened to, standing in the room. It needs no b-roll, no story input, and no rule bent. It is also the warmest thing on the account and the account is short on warmth.

Build

IMG_4679 full (0:00–0:17) as the cold open, hard cut to IMG_4680 0:19–0:41. Drop 4680's first 19 seconds — the exterior-paint and budget talk belongs in A2 and carries a dollar figure. End on "their next place." No end card beyond the standard mark; the line is the ending.

Cut the budget figure at IMG_4680 0:12 if any of this clip's head is used. De-identify absolutely: no names, no faces, no unit detail for the tenants. The story is what the company did, never who they were.

Reel A2 Nothing blocking

Walk it with me before it exists. ~50s

Standing in a closed-off floor plan, describing the open one that replaces it.

"This is gonna be an entirely open concept for a kitchen. Speaking of functionality of the house, that's what people like. When I rent my places, when I live in places, I like things to be open… That was a weird three-season room, whatever this thing is. Here's the vision for that. This wall, open it up… I think it's a three-one right now. We're making it a three-two." IMG_4681 · 0:10–0:49 · walking the ground floor
Why this one

Nobody in this market posts the vision before the work — they post the after and ask you to imagine the before. This inverts it, and the payoff reel exists automatically the moment the rehab finishes. "When I rent my places, when I live in places, I like things to be open" is him giving the actual reasoning rather than an adjective, which is the register the brand rules keep asking for.

Build

IMG_4681 0:02–0:49, cutting only the budget figure at the head. IMG_4682, the paint-day still, holds for two beats as the final frame — it is the only still in the batch and it is genuinely mid-work: drop cloths, buckets, hardware-store pails, the front door open behind it.

"Three-one to three-two" is bed-bath config language and matches the website convention exactly. Keep it. Cut the "potentially four" aside — it is speculative and the site should never carry a config that is not final.

Reel A3 Nothing blocking

Brand new windows. I have no idea how. ~50s

The upstairs tour, at speed, with the one genuinely funny beat in four minutes of footage.

"The good thing about this house, we got brand new windows. The whole house has brand new windows. I have no idea how! There's also an old telephone, so we might just keep it… New flooring. We'll get new bathroom right here. New drywall. And same with the third bathroom." IMG_4683 · 0:30–1:03 · upstairs
Why this one

"I have no idea how" is the most human line in the entire batch — a real reaction to inheriting something good in a house that is otherwise a gut job, and the kind of thing that cannot be scripted. The old wall telephone he wants to keep is the same instinct. Pair those two against the punch-list of new drywall, new floors, three new bathrooms and it is a work reel that does not read as a brag.

Build

IMG_4683 0:30–1:03. Cut the first 30 seconds — it is stair-climbing and an off-camera exchange with no information in it. This one is the fastest cut in the set: he is already moving room to room at reel pace.

Audio has another voice off-camera in the head of the clip. Trimming to 0:30 removes it, which also removes the consent question. Verify no other person is audible or visible in the final cut.

Reel A4 · blocked Breaks the money rule

The whole BRRRR, start to finish, in one minute. ~55s

He sits on the porch swing and walks through every number on the deal.

"A lot of people want to know about the financials and the BRRRR method, right, to know how that works. We ended up getting this one for [purchase]… almost 100% finance from [lender] to get a fix and flip loan… We're fixing this for [budget], so we'll be all in for [total], plus closing costs and utilities… I believe that we can get an after repair value of around [ARV]. After we recoup through refinancing, not just that we had the entire previous loan paid for, we're actually going to keep a pretty nice chunk in our hands." IMG_4684 · 0:00–0:53 · figures redacted here on purpose — see the blocker
Why it is worth fighting for

This is the single most valuable clip in the batch for the investor audience. A complete BRRRR cycle explained by an operator on a real deal, with a real lender, in under a minute, unscripted. Nothing else in the pipeline comes close for the "partner with us" side of the account, and Shavit has been asking for exactly this kind of content since 7/24.

Why it is blocked

It breaks the no-dollar-figures rule five times in fifty seconds, and the rule exists for a reason — every prior post argues on craft precisely because numbers invite scrutiny of the deal rather than the work. It also names a lender publicly, which is that firm's decision to make, not ours. Both need explicit clearance.

Fallback if the rule holds: recut as "how the BRRRR method works" with every figure replaced by a ratio or a plain-language beat — bought below value, financed the purchase, funded the rehab, refinanced out, kept the property. Same structure, same clip, no numbers. Weaker, but shippable without anyone's permission.
