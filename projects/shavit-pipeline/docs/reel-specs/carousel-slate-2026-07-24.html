00 · Why East Victoria worked, and what to keep

Before picking the next one, name what actually carried the last post, because the instinct will be to change the format. The format is not the thing to change.

  • It was a story with stakes, not a tour. Foreclosure, auction, a family, a crew, an outcome. Every slide moved the story forward instead of showing another room.
  • The before shots landed at the end. They read as proof rather than as a setup, which is the inversion that made it feel like an operator talking rather than a listing.
  • Real partners were named. Naming the lender and the general contractor is what makes a lender audience believe the rest of it.
  • The people came before the property. The slide about helping the occupants transition sat ahead of any renovation slide.

So the slate below keeps the arc and swaps the story. Brand rule ten says templated formats win. Three of these concepts reuse the East Victoria arc directly. The others exist because they say something the story arc cannot.

05 · Rules every concept above already obeys

  • No dollar figures anywhere, on screen or in caption.
  • Street names and project nicknames only, never a number leading an address.
  • Michigan, Ohio, and Indiana always spelled out.
  • Criticise the condition of a house, never the street or the neighbourhood it sits on.
  • Tenants and neighbours de-identified, crew featured only with consent.
  • Real photographs of the real property, no portal imagery and no generated architecture.
  • No contractions and no dashes in any drafted copy.
  • Two to three posts a week, ceiling, with the pinned brand film left in place.

Short answer, no. East Victoria is the only property Shavit has ever told us the story of on the record.

Checked on 2026-07-24, four places:

  • Fireflies. Eight recorded calls with him since the thirtieth of June. Only one, the Shavit Reel call on the twenty third of July, carries property story content, and its own recap tags are property acquisition and foreclosure renovation. That is the East Victoria call. Every other recorded call is website, branding, launch, or the weekly scorecard.
  • The self dictated transcript emailed on the twenty second is the scorecard call and the website walkthrough. It names East Victoria as this week's property and gives that hook. No other house.
  • The pipeline itself. No mention of Kendall, Norwood, Waldron, Howder or Lo Presto anywhere in the working files, in any story sense.
  • Texts. No Shavit thread is in scope on this machine, so nothing to mine there.

What that means practically: every concept on this board that depends on a story is bottlenecked on the same ten minute conversation, and there is no archive to fall back on. Two things follow. First, the mechanical carousel is the right post to lead with, because it is the only strong one that needs no story at all. Second, the Tuesday check in is worth restructuring: rather than asking about one house, get him talking through three, Kendall, Norwood and the duplex, and record it. One call would unblock the next month of posts instead of the next post.

Worth noting the Fireflies workspace was at ninety percent storage on the thirteenth of July and the free transcription credits are exhausted, so recordings may stop being summarised. If those calls are the only archive of his stories, that is a real risk to the content pipeline.
