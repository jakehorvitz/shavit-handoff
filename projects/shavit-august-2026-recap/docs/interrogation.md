## Evidence synthesis

### What “same thing” means

July establishes a measurable template: 1080×1920, 30 fps, approximately 63 seconds, black title card, uppercase white typography, brass accent, full-frame property sequences, restrained transitions, and a branded/team close. Shavit’s August 11 message explicitly says the template and metrics must be identical.

### What changes for August

Descriptions are not generic captions. Shavit supplied six explicit project beats and 30 corresponding images on September 4. Norwood, Oak, Ludlam, and East Ewing are progress chapters; Barry is approaching completion; Howder is complete. The on-screen prose must communicate those states precisely and, where earlier client guidance supports it, connect visible work to durable ownership rather than merely naming rooms. No partnership-impact claim was supplied in the six-project package, so none may be invented.

### What the requested “drone” and “walkthrough” language can safely mean

- Exterior: a slow rise, push, or lateral reveal beginning from a real client photograph. The camera may move; the property may not change.
- Interior: a slow push along the source photograph’s existing axis. No corner turns, doorway travel into unseen space, whip pans, or orbits.
- Text: composited in Palmier after motion generation, never generated inside the video model.
- 3D preview: approximate massing and camera frusta for blocking only, visibly labeled as a planning proxy rather than an architectural survey or a generated representation of the finished property.

### A — Monthly project ledger (recommended)

Rebuild July’s monthly recap grammar as a 55–65 second August portfolio film using Shavit’s exact numbered order: Norwood → Barry → Oak → Howder → Ludlam → East Ewing. Every chapter gets a property label, city label, and one concise progress description. The dramatic arc is active work → near-completion → completed standard → next wave of demolition → brand close.

Why it wins: it answers “same as July,” follows the exact current material package, accommodates Shavit’s requested descriptions, and lets the 3D previs demonstrate several distinct camera behaviors without making the reel feel like a single-property ad.

### B — Norwood construction story

A 35–45 second single-property progress reel moving from completed exterior envelope into newly hung drywall and stair/bath coverage.

Why it is weaker: the source images support it, but it is not an August recap and has no completed visual payoff unless Howder is introduced.

### C — Before/after standards film

A 45–60 second contrast between Norwood in progress and Howder complete, organized around “behind the walls” and “built for daily use.”

Why it is useful but secondary: strongest narrative economy, but it narrows the monthly portfolio signal and departs further from July’s multi-project metric.
