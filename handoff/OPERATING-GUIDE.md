# Running the Shavit work

## What the work is for

Shavit's brand presents a real Midwest property operator and the teams doing the work. The website supports tenants and reputation; content shows renovations, property updates, crews and completed work. Its recorded markets include Hillsdale, Michigan; Cleveland, Ohio; and South Bend, Indiana. Property-specific details and availability are dated source information, not a current inventory guarantee.

## Voice and design

- Use direct, restrained copy. State the standard and the advantage; avoid generic praise and competing-property comparisons.
- Credit the real team where appropriate. Get name-specific permission for crew features.
- Common content treatment: black canvas, white uppercase type, brass rules and gold mark, Inter. Some approved social designs use SF Pro/DM Sans substitutions. The website has its own navy/orange/gold tokens: preserve the actual implementation rather than imposing the reel palette on it.
- Use real, source-traced property photographs. Do not manufacture architectural proof or combine different houses into a before/after.
- Public content uses street names or project nicknames, full state names and verified facts. The current content pipeline prohibits dollar figures and house numbers. Full identifiers may remain in internal source filenames and stable URL slugs; their presence is not an instruction to display them publicly.
- Copy should add the WHY instead of narrating the visible WHAT. The August 25 Howder feedback is the reference for advantage-led copy.
- The current brand rules state a 2–3 posts/week ceiling. Older two-per-day and 4–6/week planning documents are historical proposals.

## Weekly working rhythm

This is a practical handoff process distilled from the original operator plan, not a claim that every integration is currently connected.

1. Collect a property set from the on-site team: before, progress, after, matching angles and a few details. Store the original files and source context together.
2. Choose the next property or team story; check the latest feedback and property facts. Keep the caption, source trace, design, review notes and export together.
3. Use the existing formats, then inspect mobile type, property identity, before/after direction, crop and sound. Avoid generating a new format when an existing one fits.
4. Get the necessary factual and owner review. Keep a small prepared buffer; publication happens through the actual account owner/operator workflow.
5. Log shipped/planned posts, reach, engagement, saves/shares, follower changes, inquiries and the next experiment. The June archive includes tracker and lead-log templates; empty templates do not prove measured performance.
