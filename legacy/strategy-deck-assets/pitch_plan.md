# Shavit Rootman — Marketing Strategy Pitch Plan

A talk track for pitching the marketing engagement to Shavit. Pairs with the 16-slide deck.
Goal of the meeting: get Shavit to say **"yes, run my marketing."**

---

## 0. The one-liner (memorize this)

> "You're a great operator with an under-leveraged digital presence. You keep doing the deals — I'll turn each one into content on LinkedIn and Instagram, in your voice, every week, and funnel the people who care to your site so investors and sellers come to **you** instead of you chasing them."

If he only remembers one sentence, it's this.

---

## 1. What Shavit has to believe by the end

A pitch works by moving someone through beliefs in order. Don't skip steps:

1. **"My online presence is weaker than my business deserves."** (the gap)
2. **"There's a real, repeatable way to fix it."** (the system)
3. **"It won't turn me into a salesy influencer or eat my time."** (the objection)
4. **"It will actually make me money — capital, deals, filled units."** (the payoff)
5. **"Jake is the person to run it."** (the ask)

Every slide should be pushing one of these five beliefs. If a slide isn't, cut it.

## 2. Slide-by-slide talk track

**1 — Title.** Set the frame: "This is a plan for how I'd run your marketing for the next year. It's built around your actual business, not generic real estate hype." Quick — don't read the slide.

**2 — The operator (who you are).** Mirror his story back to him: IDF → Hillsdale → operator, 50 doors / $12M / three states. Point: "This is a *credibility* story most investors would kill for. Right now almost nobody online knows it." Plant the gap.

**3 — The opportunity.** State it bluntly: "Great operator, thin digital footprint. ~2,600 LinkedIn followers, no consistent content. The asset is your track record — it's just invisible." This is belief #1 landing.

**4 — Three audiences.** "Everything we post is really aimed at three people: a passive investor with money, an aspiring investor who'll become your fan and amplifier, and a buyer/seller/tenant in your markets. One voice — they each self-select." Don't overcomplicate; this shows you've thought about *who*, not just *what*.

**5 — Positioning & brand voice.** The differentiator. "You're not a flipper, not a guru. Your lane is: *I take overlooked Midwest housing and turn it into long-term homes for families.* That voice is the moat — nobody in his feed sounds like a disciplined, mission-first operator." This is what makes you fundable and trustworthy.

**6 — Visual identity.** Proof of craft. "This is what your brand looks like done right — navy, cream, gold, your real photos. The deck you're looking at *is* the sample." Let the design do the talking.

**7 — Channel strategy.** Each channel has ONE job (say it exactly like this):
- LinkedIn = top of funnel (where investors/operators are)
- Substack = depth + trust (turns a follower into someone who'll wire money)
- Website = conversion (everything points here)
- Instagram = proof + human texture (before/afters, the work)

**8 — LinkedIn plan.** Concrete cadence: a few posts/week, built on content pillars (deal breakdowns, lessons, market takes, mission/community). "Build in public on the rails you already have (~2,631 followers)." Concreteness = credibility.

**9 — Substack plan.** "This is where the real trust gets built — long-form on how the BRRRR + execution-sheet system actually works. This is what converts attention into capital."

**10 — Content engine (the key slide — slow down here).** "Here's why this is sustainable and not a treadmill: you *already generate the content by doing your job.* Every BRRRR cycle, every Hillsdale rehab, every system you run is a repeatable series. I'm documenting work that's already happening — you don't have to become a content creator." This kills belief #3 (the time/effort objection).

**11 — Website as conversion engine.** Transition from "attention" to "money." (Full detail in §3 below.) "Posts build trust; the site closes. Seven pages, two buttons, everything optimized to turn a curious follower into an inquiry."

**12 — The funnel.** One picture: Audience (LinkedIn/IG) → Trust (Substack) → Site → Inquiry (Work With Me / Invest With Me). "This is the whole machine on one slide."

**13 — 90-day rollout.** Phase it: Days 1–30 foundation (voice, visual identity, cadence, site polish), 31–60 publish + build momentum, 61–90 optimize + first measurable results. "By day 90 I show you numbers, not vibes."

**14 — KPIs & reporting.** *(needs his/your real targets — see §5.)* "Here's how we'll know it's working: follower growth, reach, and — the one that matters — qualified inquiries and investor conversations."

**15 — The ask.** Scope, cadence, investment, next step. Be direct: "Here's what I'd run, here's what it costs, here's what you do today to start." (See §6.)

**16 — Close.** Restate the mission line. End on *his* words, not yours.

## 3. The website — what's marketable (page by page)

The site is where attention becomes money, so it has to *sell*, not just inform. Two CTAs only — **Work With Me** and **Invest With Me** — repeated everywhere. Every page answers a visitor's silent question: *"Why should I trust this guy with my money or my house?"*

- **Home** — the hook. Mission line + the proof numbers above the fold (50 doors · $12M+ · 3 states · 200 by 2030). Both CTAs visible immediately. A visitor should "get it" in 5 seconds.
- **About / The Operator** — the trust story. IDF discipline → Hillsdale → operator. This page does the emotional heavy lifting; it's *why* an investor picks him over a faceless fund.
- **Track Record / Portfolio** — the receipts. Real before/after transformations, real markets (Hillsdale, Cleveland, South Bend), the flagship (17 Lo Presto Ave). Photos > paragraphs. **This is the single most marketable page** — it's proof, not promises.
- **Invest With Me** — the money page. Who he works with, the BRRRR thesis in plain English, what a partnership looks like, what returns are based on. Soft, credible, not hypey. CTA: a short inquiry form (no sensitive financial info collected on the site — just "let's talk").
- **Work With Me** — for buyers/sellers/tenants in his markets. "Have a property in Hillsdale/Cleveland/South Bend? Let's talk." Local deal flow.
- **The Vision / Community** — 200 doors by 2030, 100 homes revitalized in Hillsdale, 30 local jobs. This is what makes him *likable* and different from a yield-chaser. Mission sells.
- **Contact** — frictionless. Both CTAs, email, phone. Never a dead end.

**Marketable threads to hit everywhere:** the numbers (concrete = credible), the before/afters (transformation is inherently shareable), the mission ("long-term homes for families" — emotional + differentiated), and the discipline angle (IDF/execution-systems = "this guy won't lose my money"). Avoid: hype, "passive income" guru language, anything that smells like a course.

## 4. How it makes money (the profitability logic)

Marketing isn't a cost here — it's a cheaper way to do things he already pays for. Three concrete money levers:

**Lever 1 — Cheaper, faster capital.** His business runs on raising money for deals. Today that's 1-on-1 hustle. A trust engine (LinkedIn + Substack proving the track record) means investors come to *him* warm and pre-sold. One extra investor relationship funded off content can be worth six figures of deployable capital. Content lowers his cost of capital — that's the whole game in real estate.

**Lever 2 — Off-market deal flow.** Buyers/sellers/wholesalers in Hillsdale/Cleveland/South Bend who follow him bring him deals before they hit the market. Better deals at better prices = more margin per door. The "Work With Me" funnel turns audience into pipeline.

**Lever 3 — Lower vacancy / faster lease-up.** A known local brand fills units faster. Every week a door sits empty is lost rent; brand awareness shortens that. Small per-door, real at 50→200 doors.

**The math to say out loud:** "If a year of consistent content lands you even *one* new investor or *two* off-market deals you wouldn't have gotten otherwise, it's paid for itself many times over. And unlike paid ads, the content compounds — it keeps working after it's posted." Frame it as **ROI, not expense.**

**Profitable for Jake too (the engagement):** retainer for running socials + website + content. Priced so it's trivial against one funded deal. *(Jake to set the actual number — see §5/§6.)*

## 5. Numbers you need to fill in before pitching

The deck has these marked `[FILL IN]`. Decide them so the plan reads as a real commitment, not a hope:

- **KPI targets at 90 days / 6 months:** e.g. follower growth %, posts/week committed, # qualified inquiries, # of investor conversations. Even rough ranges.
- **Current baselines:** anything beyond the ~2,631 LinkedIn followers (IG, email list, site traffic) so growth is measurable.
- **Your fee / engagement structure:** monthly retainer? scope tiers? what's included.

Want me to draft realistic target *ranges* for the KPIs so you have defensible numbers to say?

## 6. The ask & close

- **Be specific about scope:** "I run LinkedIn + Instagram + Substack + the website. You give me access to what you're already doing — deal updates, photos, a 15-min voice memo a week — and I turn it into everything."
- **State the investment plainly** (don't bury it). Confidence sells.
- **Make the next step tiny:** "All I need to start is access to the accounts and a walkthrough of one current deal. I'll have the first week of content live in [X] days."
- **Close on his mission**, in his voice — not on you. The last thing he hears should be why *his* work matters.

---

## 7. Likely objections (have answers ready)

- *"I don't have time for content."* → That's the whole point of the engine (slide 10): you do the deals, I do the content. ~15 min/week from you.
- *"I don't want to look salesy / like a guru."* → Your positioning is the opposite — disciplined operator, mission-first. The voice is the moat.
- *"Does this actually make money?"* → §4. One funded investor or off-market deal pays for a year. It compounds; ads don't.
- *"Why you?"* → You already manage it, you built this plan and the brand sample, and you understand the business — not a generic agency.
