## LI-1 — The thesis post
**Angle:** Authority / brand foundation · pairs with portfolio montage or 17 Lo Presto exterior

**Hook:**
> Everyone wants the asset. Almost nobody wants to operate it. That gap is the whole business.

**Body:**
Five years ago, a conversation in a renovated carriage house at Hillsdale College turned into a thesis I never let go of: housing, done with discipline and built around the community, can transform a town.

Today that's 50+ doors and a $12M+ portfolio across three states — Michigan, Ohio, Indiana. The strategy is BRRRR, but run differently. We buy directly from owners, with no agents on either side. We rebuild with our own crews, tracked weekly across every trade until the work is done. We hold under our own management. Each completed cycle becomes its own company.

None of that is glamorous. All of it compounds.

The phrase we operate by is "building communities, profitably." Most people hear those two words as opposites. Run right, each one funds the other — the cash flow pays for the standard, and the standard is what makes a town want you there for the next twenty years.

## LI-3 — Market read: small markets reward operators
**Angle:** Thought-leadership / market read · pairs with Hillsdale or South Bend streetscape

**Hook:**
> We deliberately operate in markets most institutional capital won't touch. That's not a compromise. It's the edge.

**Body:**
Hillsdale. Jackson County. Cleveland. South Bend. The markets are chosen on purpose — large enough to scale, small enough to know personally.

These markets reward operators with infrastructure on the ground and punish anyone underwriting from a distance. A spreadsheet in another state can't see the deferred maintenance behind the drywall, can't read which block is turning, can't get a crew there on Tuesday. We can, because we live in the work.

Cleveland is a good example of reading the gap. We run furnished mid-term housing near the hospital systems and universities — 30 to 180 day stays for the medical corridor, traveling professionals, grad students. The mispricing sits in the space between hotel inventory and a 12-month lease. The operation lives in that gap.

That's the whole philosophy: you don't beat institutional capital on price. You beat it on proximity.

How we think about all three states: shavitrootman.com.

## LI-4 — Lesson from the field: "not all real estate is sexy"
**Angle:** Lessons / values · pairs with **Second Chance housing reel/photos**

**Hook:**
> The best deal we did last cycle started with an eviction, an ozone machine, and a house cleaned down to the studs.

**Body:**
Not all real estate is sexy.

We acquired a property with a difficult tenant situation and serious deferred maintenance — the kind of file most buyers close and walk away from. We worked through the eviction. We ran ozone treatment. We cleaned it down to the studs and put it back on the market. It's now stabilized with a new long-term family in it.

We call this second-chance housing, and it's most of what we actually do in markets like South Bend — the distressed, overlooked homes others walk past: bad histories, deferred maintenance, sellers who need an exit before foreclosure.

The numbers work for one boring reason: we showed up and did the work. There's no clever financing that substitutes for that. The willingness to do the unglamorous part *is* the moat.

## LI-5 — Seller experience / reputation: Jeffrey Riling
**Angle:** Reputation / trust · pairs with Jackson County direct-purchase photo

**Hook:**
> A veteran sold us his property, transferred his loan to us, and then took the time to recommend us publicly. That's the metric I actually watch.

**Body:**
We buy directly from owners — no agents on either side. On paper that's a structure: a loan assumption, a 60-day close, fees that stay in the deal instead of leaving it. In practice it's a relationship, because the seller is trusting you with something that has their name on it.

Jeffrey S. Riling, a US veteran, sold us a Jackson County property and handled the loan assumption with us. Here's what he said afterward:

*"Professional across all aspects of his work. What stands out is the kindness and camaraderie he brings to the table, making you feel like part of the family."*

In a direct-purchase model, your reputation is the deal flow. There's no broker in the middle to absorb a bad experience — it's just you and the person across the table. So we treat "would they recommend us" as a leading indicator, not a nice-to-have.

What partners and sellers say, in their words: shavitrootman.com/case-studies.
